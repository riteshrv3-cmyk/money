# The Politics Book — Curriculum v2 (architecture edition)

Goal: the complete Indian political machine as ONE connected system in Ritesh's head — every player's incentives closed, every money loop traced, every level linked to the next. Like the money-map, but for power.

Design spine (all chapters):
- **Master map**: one ASCII system-map of the whole machine, drawn inside each chapter. Starts as one box in ch.1, grows every chapter, complete by ch.21. You always see where you are in the architecture.
- **Incentive equation**: every chapter must answer "why is this behavior rational for this player?" No behavior left unexplained.
- **Contested models**: where politics has no settled truth, the 2-3 competing explanations are shown side by side and marked as contested.
- **The character**: one fictional neta's life runs through the whole book — a village boy in ch.1 who climbs exactly as the chapters climb (sarpanch by Part 1, MLA through Part 2, party player in Part 3, brushes the top floors in Part 4, and is mapped himself in ch.21). The architecture is watched through one life.
- **Self-test**: every chapter ends with 3-5 from-your-head questions. Can't answer = reread. After the final chapter: 2-3 real-seat case files as appendices, applying the full architecture to real constituencies.
- Style: field manual with village first-principles intros, Hinglish, ASCII diagrams, real numbers (hard numbers verified — salaries, funds, caps; ground realities labeled reported/estimate), real careers as illustrations of mechanisms (giants, unknown MLAs, climbers, failures), fully honest including grey economy (analytical, no how-to), no party bias. Each chapter ends with "What this means for you."

## Part 0 — The Operating System (why the machine exists)

1. **Raja kyun chahiye** — Governance derived as a need: disputes, protection, shared resources; why the village must hire a decider, and what the decider earns instead of salary.
   *(Clarity: the foundation stone. Every later behavior — corruption, dynasty, favors — stops looking like evil and starts looking like the predictable economics of this one strange job: massive power, tiny salary. Nothing above makes sense without this.)*

2. **Vote ki keemat** — The vote as currency: why one vote is worthless and a vote-BANK is everything; the arithmetic (turnout, margins, splits) that silently rules every decision above it.
   *(Clarity: gives you the unit of account of the whole system. Once you see that every player is doing vote-bank math, every "irrational" political move becomes a calculation you can redo yourself.)*

## Part 1 — The Machine's Smallest Parts

3. **Sarpanch ka election** — Politics at 1,000 voters: family blocks, money, panchayat funds — the whole system in miniature.
   *(Clarity: like learning the cell before the body. Every mechanism of national politics — vote-buying, caste blocks, fund capture — exists complete at village scale, small enough to hold in your head at once.)*

4. **Corporator** — The same cell in a city: wards, municipal budgets, the builder nexus.
   *(Clarity: shows the machine is scale-free — same architecture, bigger money. After this you can predict what any political unit looks like at any size.)*

5. **Karyakarta** — Worker economics: why thousands work "free," what loyalty costs, what the machine's payroll really is.
   *(Clarity: solves the system's biggest visible mystery — who does all this work and why. The answer (hope + protection + identity + dandha) is the glue holding every layer together. Miss this and the whole machine looks impossible.)*

## Part 2 — The MLA (the machine's central gear)

6. **Seat ki anatomy** — What an MLA actually is: 2.5-3 lakh voters, the assembly role vs the real job.
   *(Clarity: corrects the single biggest public misunderstanding — that MLAs make laws. Seeing the REAL job description makes everything an MLA does suddenly coherent.)*

7. **Ticket** — The election inside the party: surveys, loyalty, money, caste, winnability.
   *(Clarity: reveals the system's hidden bottleneck. The real competition is 3,000 aspirants for 288 tickets, not candidate vs candidate. Once you see the ticket market, party behavior decodes itself.)*

8. **Campaign ka paisa aur machine** — Real cost vs the ₹40 lakh cap; where money actually comes from; the calendar; the booth system person by person.
   *(Clarity: closes the system's biggest money loop — election spending is an INVESTMENT, and once you trace who invests and what return they expect, the corruption architecture explains itself without any moralizing.)*

9. **Election day** — The ground war hour by hour: booth pramukhs, agents, turnout ops, counting.
   *(Clarity: shows where all the machinery from ch.3-8 actually discharges. The abstract vote-bank math becomes physical: this many workers, this many booths, these exact hours. The system's moment of truth.)*

10. **Paanch saal** — The years between: janta darbar, event attendance as the job, office and staff, the favor economy, MLALADS.
    *(Clarity: explains the system's steady state — what the machine does when nobody is watching. The favor economy chapter shows you politics' real daily currency: not money, not votes, but favors that convert into both.)*

11. **MLA ka business model** — How political wealth actually builds: institutions (colleges, co-ops, sugar mills), contracts, land, influence.
    *(Clarity: completes the MLA as an economic unit — the career P&L. Also decodes your own MLA: the college is not a side business, it IS the political model. After this chapter you can read any politician's asset list like a balance sheet.)*

## Part 3 — The Machine Above the Machine (the party)

12. **High command aur gut** — Factions, state leadership, who really controls tickets and posts.
    *(Clarity: adds the vertical dimension — every MLA is simultaneously a king below and a servant above. Power flows down as tickets/posts, loyalty flows up. This dual role explains most "strange" MLA behavior.)*

13. **Dal-badal** — Defection: the anti-defection law, resort politics, the switching math.
    *(Clarity: the system under stress. Defection is where every hidden incentive becomes visible at once — you literally see the price of loyalty. Best single window into what MLAs are really worth and to whom.)*

14. **Ticket se bada banna** — Personal vote banks, dynasty, institutions: becoming un-droppable.
    *(Clarity: explains the system's long game — how a player converts one seat into permanent family infrastructure. Answers why dynasty is not a bug but the equilibrium outcome of the machine's own rules.)*

## Part 4 — The Top Floors

15. **MP** — Delhi's different game: MP vs MLA, MPLADS, weaker at home, stronger in Delhi.
    *(Clarity: prevents the classic error of treating MP as "bigger MLA." It's a different job with different physics — seeing the contrast sharpens both models in your head.)*

16. **Mantri aur CM** — Power converted: portfolios, the transfer-posting economy, the minister-secretary-collector triangle.
    *(Clarity: shows what winning is FOR. Government power = the ability to move officials and sign files, and the transfer-posting economy is where political power converts back into money and control. Closes the power loop.)*

17. **Paise ka rasta** — Centre → State → constituency: schemes, funds, every hand the money passes through.
    *(Clarity: the master money diagram. One chapter, one river: where government money originates and every point where it can be diverted. After this, every scheme announcement on the news becomes legible.)*

## Part 5 — The Forces That Move the Whole Machine

18. **Jaat ka ganit** — Caste and community arithmetic, head-on: tickets, alliances, booth strategy.
    *(Clarity: the system's deepest variable. Caste is the pre-installed vote-bank software every player must compute with. Treating it head-on, as math rather than taboo, is what separates real understanding from news-level understanding.)*

19. **Lehar, media, WhatsApp** — Waves, ground machine vs media, digital war rooms; what tech changes and what it doesn't. Contested-models chapter: the 2-3 competing theories of what actually wins elections, shown honestly.
    *(Clarity: teaches you where the map itself is fuzzy. Knowing which questions have NO settled answer — and what each camp believes — is veteran-level clarity; false certainty here is how outsiders expose themselves.)*

20. **Careers ki maut** — How careers end: lost tickets, one-term wonders, defections gone wrong.
    *(Clarity: failure cases are the system's proof. Each dead career shows a rule of the machine by what happens when it's violated. Survivorship-only understanding is half understanding.)*

## Closing

21. **Das minute ka naksha** — The synthesis: the complete master map assembled, plus the method — level, power source, risk, need — for placing any politician on it in 10 minutes.
    *(Clarity: the compression test. If the book worked, the entire machine now fits on one page and any new political fact you ever hear has an obvious slot. This chapter is the proof-of-clarity.)*
