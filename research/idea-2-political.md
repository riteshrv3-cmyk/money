# Idea 2: Political campaign software for MLA/MP candidates

Full sourced raw research: [research/raw/political.md](raw/political.md). Day 2 adversarial refutation applied below.

## Scores (frozen rubric)

**Ranking A — speed to first ₹1: 4.0 / 5**
Highest of the four on paper — Ritesh has direct, personal access to a sitting MLA/MP, no gatekeeper, no cold outreach needed. A decision-maker who trusts him personally can say yes in days, not months.

**Ranking B — ceiling: 3.0 / 5**
Ticket size can be real (₹5L-2cr range reported for the industry), and the software itself has leverage (a voter-data/war-room dashboard is reusable across candidates). But the buyer pool is small: **only ~100 potential candidates per state actually hire outside help**, out of ~700 who are even viable candidates. The top of that pool (I-PAC scale) is already locked up by a decade-old incumbent with 500-1,000+ employees.

## What survived adversarial refutation

- **No evidence exists of any solo developer receiving a standing monthly retainer from an individual MLA/MP.** Confirmed on all 3 lenses — targeted searches for exactly this arrangement found only firms and packaged products, never a solo dev on retainer. This is the single biggest unvalidated assumption in the plan: the revenue model itself has zero precedent, so it must be tested directly with Ritesh's contact, not assumed.
- **A direct competitor (Pollity) already exists, doing exactly the proposed pilot.** Confirmed on all 3 lenses by visiting pollity.in directly: it markets itself as a "Digital Chief of Staff" for MPs/MLAs — grievance tracking, MPLADS/MLALADS fund dashboards, constituency staff coordination, auto-generated social content — the identical non-election-year use case, positioned explicitly against expensive consultancies. It states it is "launching Q3 2026." **This is the single most important fact in this entire research round**: someone else, apparently with real resources, is about to launch the exact product idea, with no public pricing yet.

## What got knocked down

- **"Political consulting shows severe price erosion (Rs 100 → Rs 10)"** — majority-refuted on logical-consistency grounds. This rests on one named consultant's single pricing anecdote, generalized into a market-wide trend claim. Real, quotable, but thinner evidence than it first appears.
- **"Addressable market is only ~700 viable candidates / ~100 buyers per state"** — majority-refuted the same way: a single anonymous consultant's estimate, treated as a hard market-sizing number. Directionally useful (this is clearly a small-buyer-pool market) but shouldn't be taken as precise.

Net effect of the refutation on these two: the "market is small and price-eroded" story is *probably true* (both lenses corroborate each other) but rests on thinner sourcing than a first read suggests — worth a direct gut-check with Ritesh's own MLA/MP contact rather than treating it as settled.

## Fatal flaw

**Timing, not size.** A funded-looking competitor is about to launch the identical pilot product this quarter, into a buyer pool of roughly 100 people per state who already have limited discretionary spend and, per the ground-operations data, prioritize manpower over software 40% vs 25% of budget. If Ritesh wants this dot, the test has to happen **now**, before Pollity's Q3 2026 launch closes the window — and the very first move should be a direct conversation with his MLA/MP contact about what Pollity offers and whether it beats a solo alternative, not a build-first approach.
