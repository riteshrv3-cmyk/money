# Idea 1: Real estate agent, online-only, no network, no capital

Full sourced raw research: [research/raw/real-estate.md](raw/real-estate.md). Day 2 adversarial refutation applied below (3 independent lenses per claim: source quality, does-it-fit-solo-India, logical consistency; majority of 3 decides).

## Scores (frozen rubric)

**Ranking A — speed to first ₹1: 2.5 / 5**
Payer identifiable this week (yes, any buyer/seller/renter) and decision-maker reachable solo (yes) — but RERA agent registration is legally mandatory before you can earn commission on any registered project (₹10,590 fee, ~30-day approval in Maharashtra), and property deals close in weeks-to-months, not days.

**Ranking B — ceiling: 2.5 / 5**
Ticket size is real (builder commissions 1-4%) and buyer count is large, but **leverage is 1/5** — this is a labor business. Every deal requires fresh personal effort; nothing you build compounds without turning into a different business (property software), which is a different arrow entirely. No evidence anyone reached $10M+ ARR as a solo online agent.

## What survived adversarial refutation

- **RERA registration is a real, mandatory gate** (confirmed on all 3 lenses) — ₹10,590 fee, ~30 days, applies identically regardless of capital or team size.
- **Commission structure holds** (confirmed on all 3 lenses): resale 1-2%, rental ~1 month's rent, builder/developer deals 1-4% with the *developer* paying (not the buyer) — channel partners still source 40-70% of bookings on a typical builder project.
- **No India-specific data exists on first-year income for a zero-network solo online agent.** One adversarial lens pushed back usefully here: the "you need 12-24 months of network-building" conclusion was borrowed from a US blog, and US real-estate economics (MLS access, broker sponsorship, referral culture) don't map cleanly onto India's more informal, WhatsApp/portal-lead-driven market. **This means the zero-network premise is genuinely untested here, not proven to fail** — it's an open question, not a closed one.

## What got knocked down

- **"NoBroker proves major platforms are built to eliminate agents"** — refuted on logical-consistency grounds: this generalizes from one company (NoBroker) to "the largest platforms" as a category, when 99acres/MagicBricks/Housing.com actually operate as broker-dependent marketplaces. The real threat isn't platform-wide disintermediation — it's that channel partners still capture 40-70% of builder bookings, meaning the segment isn't dead, but a solo entrant enters as one voice among many established brokers already inside that channel.

## Fatal flaw

Not legal, not market size — **structural**. This is a service business where your own labor is the product. It doesn't scale without hiring, and it doesn't fit the bootstrapped-SaaS math that makes $100M cash arithmetically possible ([money-map.md](../money-map.md)). Ritesh already floated this as a possible 6-month *learning* route rather than an end business — the research supports treating it exactly that way, not as the primary bet.
